## Why this idea

Autonomous software can search, reason, call APIs, and transact, but it cannot directly observe whether a product is actually on a shelf, what price is physically displayed, whether a promotion is correctly executed, or whether an asset or site is in the claimed condition. Digital sources often describe historical or administrative state rather than present physical reality.

Ravi's background spans production backend systems, onchain settlement, autonomous-agent payments, and earlier work involving sensor and physical-maintenance data. GroundTruth applies that combination to a narrow operating problem: translate a machine request into an objective field protocol, dispatch a human operator, review fresh evidence, and return a machine-readable result.

Need has not yet been proven through paid commercial usage. The current evidence is a live product, working payment and task interfaces, and early external interest/outreach. The accelerator plan must therefore prioritize paid pilots and measured fulfillment rather than claim product-market fit.

## Market opportunity

GroundTruth should not lead with an unsupported top-down TAM. The initial measurable market is outlet-level retail execution verification for emerging consumer brands: on-shelf availability, displayed price, promotion, facings, and shelf placement. The expansion path includes procurement, logistics, property, equipment, and real-world-asset workflows whose software systems need current physical evidence before acting.

The near-term proof is bottom-up: sell two $199 launch pilots, measure operator and review economics, convert at least two buyers to $249 or $599 monthly plans, and exceed $1,000 MRR through external paid activity.

## Competition and differentiation

Alternatives include conventional retail-audit firms, mystery-shopping vendors, generic gig marketplaces, manual staff checks, web search, and emerging human-task APIs. GroundTruth is not defensible merely because it has an endpoint or accepts photos.

The intended differentiation is an operating layer for machine-originated physical verification: objective proof specifications, asynchronous status, Base USDC/x402 payment, machine-readable evidence receipts, operator reliability history, acceptance rules, and buyer-specific schemas. These advantages remain hypotheses until repeat paid fulfillment proves them.

## Business model

- $199 launch pilot for 25 accepted retail checks in one confirmed operating zone.
- $249/month for 25 accepted checks.

## Go-to-market strategy

1. Start with one repeatable retail question in a compact Hyderabad operating zone.
2. Recruit and calibrate a small operator pool before promising coverage.
3. Sell two paid $199 pilots to emerging consumer brands and retail-operations teams.
4. Publish real completion-time, first-pass acceptance, exception, payout, and review-cost metrics.
5. Convert successful pilots to recurring plans.
6. Add a genuine Fetch/uAgents/Agentverse adapter so Fetch agents can discover the service, create an asynchronous verification mission, observe status, and consume a structured result.
7. Use demonstrated outcomes—not generic agent-directory listings—to expand to procurement, logistics, and real-world-asset workflows.

## Five-year vision

GroundTruth becomes the permissioned physical-verification layer that autonomous software calls before acting on a consequential physical assumption. A buyer or agent submits an objective evidence specification; GroundTruth finds qualified local capacity, manages proof collection and review, and returns a verifiable receipt with transparent cost, provenance, and confidence boundaries. The long-term moat is coverage intelligence, operator reliability, evidence protocols, and repeatable fulfillment economics—not a generic task marketplace.
